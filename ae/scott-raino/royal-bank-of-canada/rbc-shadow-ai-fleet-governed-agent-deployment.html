Eliminating Shadow AI at RBC: How Fleet Gives Business Lines a Governed Path to Deploy Agents

Every large bank is managing the same quiet problem: business lines aren't waiting for central engineering to build AI tooling. Wealth management ops teams are experimenting with consumer-grade LLM tools. Capital markets analysts are piping client data into browser-based AI assistants. Compliance teams are building unofficial GPT wrappers to summarize regulatory updates. The tools are proliferating faster than the controls — and at RBC's scale, that's a material operational risk.

The Shadow AI Problem Is a Governance Gap, Not a People Problem

The underlying driver isn't recklessness — it's that the path from "I need an AI tool" to "I have an approved, IT-governed AI tool" is too long and too opaque for most business units. When the alternative is a six-month engineering queue, teams find workarounds. The answer isn't a blanket prohibition; it's collapsing the gap between what's fast and what's governed.

LangChain's Fleet product is designed precisely for this. It gives non-engineering teams — wealth management, capital markets ops, compliance, HR — a no-code interface to configure and deploy internal agents, while enforcing the same RBAC, audit logs, and SSO-backed identity controls that production engineering systems use. Business users get autonomy; the security and compliance org gets visibility and control.

What This Looks Like for RBC's Compliance Team

Take a practical example: RBC's compliance function needs an agent that monitors internal policy documents, surfaces relevant updates when new OSFI guidance drops, and answers advisor questions about current suitability rules. Today, that either sits in an engineering backlog or gets built informally with no audit trail.

With Fleet, a compliance analyst can configure that agent — connecting it to approved internal knowledge bases, scoping it to specific user groups via RBAC, and enabling full logging of every query and response — without writing a line of code. The agent runs on the same LangSmith-backed infrastructure as production systems, so every interaction is traceable and every access control is enforced at the platform level.

The Signal You Didn't Know You Were Missing

There's a secondary benefit that's easy to underestimate. When Credit Genie deployed their AskGenie financial assistant on LangSmith, the Insights Agent surfaced that 36% of conversations represented unmet support needs the product team hadn't identified. At RBC, the equivalent signal — advisors repeatedly asking an agent questions it can't answer, or compliance users hitting the same knowledge gaps — directly informs product roadmap and flags potential regulatory exposure from unhandled queries before they become incidents.

Governed deployment isn't just about risk mitigation. At scale, it's also your best source of ground-truth data about what your people actually need from AI — and that intelligence only exists if the tooling is centralized enough to observe it.
