How Multi-Agent Routing Can Fix FanDuel's Most Expensive Support Bottlenecks

When a bettor's withdrawal is frozen thirty minutes before kickoff, they're not submitting a support ticket — they're rage-quitting. At FanDuel's scale, millions of those moments happen every week, and the difference between a resolved issue and a churned user often comes down to how fast the right answer reaches the right person.

The Problem with Single-Agent Support

Most AI support implementations bolt a single LLM onto a help center. It's fine for FAQs. It breaks immediately when a user asks why their same-game parlay was voided and why their bonus balance didn't apply and whether their account is flagged. That's three different systems, three different data sources, and a conversation that needs to stay coherent across all of them.

A single-agent architecture can't route intelligently between those domains without hallucinating a confident-sounding wrong answer — which, in a regulated gambling context, is worse than no answer at all.

Multi-Agent Routing with LangGraph

LangGraph's orchestration model is built for exactly this decomposition. A supervisor agent classifies the incoming intent — bet dispute, withdrawal hold, responsible gambling flag, promo inquiry — and routes to a specialized sub-agent with the right tools and context window for that domain. Each sub-agent can call internal APIs, query bet settlement logs, or trigger a compliance workflow independently.

Critically, LangGraph's durable execution means a long-running resolution flow — say, a dispute that requires pulling game data, checking house rules, and escalating to a human trader — doesn't lose state if the process is interrupted. The graph resumes exactly where it left off.

# Simplified LangGraph supervisor pattern
from langgraph.graph import StateGraph

builder = StateGraph(SupportState)
builder.add_node("classifier", classify_intent)
builder.add_node("bet_dispute_agent", handle_bet_dispute)
builder.add_node("withdrawal_agent", handle_withdrawal)
builder.add_node("rg_agent", handle_responsible_gambling)
builder.add_conditional_edges("classifier", route_by_intent)

The Klarna Benchmark

Klarna deployed a similar multi-agent support architecture and handled 2.5 million conversations in its first month — work that previously required 700 full-time agents — with an 80% reduction in resolution time. The sports betting context is arguably higher stakes: Klarna transactions are asynchronous, FanDuel bets are live. The speed and accuracy bar is higher, which makes the case for purpose-built agent routing even stronger.

What This Looks Like in Practice

  • A deposit failure at 8:57 PM ET on a Sunday gets triaged, diagnosed against payment processor logs, and resolved — or escalated with full context — in under 90 seconds.
  • A same-game parlay dispute routes to a sub-agent that has read access to real-time scoring data and settlement rules, not a generic knowledge base.
  • A responsible gambling intervention triggers a separate, carefully scoped agent with its own guardrails and audit trail — fully isolated from the commercial support flow.

The engineering lift to get there isn't a rewrite. LangGraph runs on top of your existing stack, and the open-source core means the team can prototype the routing graph before any commercial commitment is on the table.
