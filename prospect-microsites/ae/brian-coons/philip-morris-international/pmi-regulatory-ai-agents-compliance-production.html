From Pilot to Production: How PMI Can Scale Regulatory AI Agents Without Creating a Compliance Liability

Philip Morris International operates across more than 180 markets, each with its own health authority, submission format, and regulatory clock. For a company in the middle of one of the most ambitious product transformations in consumer goods history — pivoting an entire portfolio toward reduced-risk products like IQOS and ZYN — the volume and complexity of regulatory filings is not a background task. It is a core operational constraint.

The question most AI teams at heavily regulated companies eventually face is not can we automate this workflow — it's how do we prove the agent is right before it touches something that matters?

The Eval-Driven Deployment Model

LangChain's approach to this problem is evaluation before deployment, not monitoring after failure. Using LangSmith's eval framework, PMI's regulatory affairs engineering team can define ground-truth datasets drawn directly from previously approved submissions — structured scientific summaries, toxicological assessments, clinical study reports — and run every new agent version against those benchmarks before it reaches a production workflow.

This matters because regulatory submissions are not forgiving of hallucination. A single factual inconsistency in an FDA Modified Risk Tobacco Product Application (MRTPA) or a WHO Framework Convention filing can trigger a Request for Information that sets a market entry timeline back by months. Eval-gating creates a documented, auditable threshold that an agent must clear before it is authorized to draft, synthesize, or route a submission artifact.

Governance That Matches the Scrutiny PMI Already Lives Under

LangChain's enterprise governance stack was built for environments where accountability is non-negotiable. For PMI's deployment context, the relevant controls include:

  • SOC 2 Type II certification — independently verified security and availability controls
  • RBAC and ABAC — role and attribute-based access so a regulatory analyst in Japan sees only the market-relevant document corpus, not the global submission archive
  • Full audit logs — every agent action, every document retrieved, every human approval captured and queryable
  • EU data residency and self-hosted Kubernetes on AWS, GCP, or Azure — meaning PMI can keep submission data inside the jurisdictions that require it

The Confidence to Scale

C.H. Robinson used this same eval-driven pattern to move from a logistics automation pilot to processing 5,500 orders per day with confidence. The principle translates directly to PMI's regulatory context: define what correct looks like, test against it continuously, and give leadership a quantitative basis for expanding agent scope rather than an intuition.

For a company under constant regulatory scrutiny, that quantitative basis is not a nice-to-have. It is the condition under which any serious AI deployment can actually be approved internally.
